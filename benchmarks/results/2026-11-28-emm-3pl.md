# Mixture (EMM) M-step for 3PL / 4PL: `BAEM(asymptote_m_step="mixture")` vs. `"joint"`

Measured 2026-11-28 on a 16-core Intel Xeon (2.80 GHz) with `JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu`, by
`benchmarks/emm_3pl.py` (issue #165). The small-sample runs used 8 processes at once with one XLA
thread each; the speed runs ran alone. `dloglik_vs_joint` is the log-likelihood of the stopped fit
minus that of `"joint"` with the same settings.

Summary: both M-steps have the same maximum, but the mixture M-step converges much more slowly
(the extra latent state adds missing information, so the EM rate is closer to 1): 4–60× more
iterations and 3–40× the wall time. At the same `tol` it therefore stops farther from the maximum
(lower log-likelihood). In small samples it did not converge more often than `"joint"`; its
smaller RMSEs and fewer boundary asymptotes come from stopping before the maximum, not from a
better estimator. `"joint"` stays the default.
